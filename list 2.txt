2. First explain the real-world problem
You can start your meeting with this explanation:

"Before discussing the technology, I want to explain a real problem in the insurance domain.

When a customer applies for life or health insurance, the insurance company needs to understand the customer's details, medical history, lifestyle and risk factors before issuing a policy.

Currently, underwriters may need to review multiple documents manually, such as application forms, medical reports, laboratory reports and identity documents.

For example, a customer might declare that they are a non-smoker in the application form, but another document may contain information that needs further verification.

Similarly, some medical reports may be missing, customer details may not match across documents, or important information may be difficult to identify.

This creates manual work, delays in processing applications and the possibility of missing important information.

So, my idea is to develop a system that assists underwriters by reading documents, checking information, applying business rules and identifying cases that need human review."

5. What important calculations are required?
This is an important part of your meeting. An insurance application is not just document reading. Insurance involves financial calculations, medical measurements and risk evaluation.

Here are the main calculations you should understand.
A. Customer and medical calculations
Calculation	Formula / purpose
Customer age	Current date − date of birth
BMI	Weight (kg) ÷ Height (m)²
Age at policy commencement	Policy start date − date of birth
Medical reference comparison	Compare extracted values with approved medical reference ranges
Medical discrepancy	Compare declared information with verified document information

Example BMI:

A customer weighs 80 kg and has a height of 1.75 m.
\[
BMI=\frac{80}{1.75\times1.75}=26.12
\]

Illustrative calculation only. BMI alone must not determine insurance eligibility.
B. Insurance financial calculations
Calculation	Why it matters
Sum assured	Determines the agreed coverage
Base premium	Calculates the starting premium
Additional premium/loading	May account for additional assessed risk
Discount	Applies approved discounts
Final premium	Amount payable by the customer
Policy term	Duration of insurance coverage

An illustrative premium calculation:
\[
\text{Final Premium} =
\text{Base Premium}+\text{Loading}-\text{Discount}
\]

For example:
- Base premium = ₹12,000
- Additional loading = ₹2,000
- Discount = ₹1,000
\[
12,000+2,000-1,000=\boxed{₹13,000}
\]

Actual premium rates and loading rules must come from the insurer's approved pricing and actuarial guidelines. The project should not invent them.
C. Risk assessment calculations
A prototype could use a weighted risk score.

For example:
Risk factor	Illustrative points
Missing medical information	10
Unverified declaration	15
Document discrepancy	20
Additional medical review required	25

\[
\text{Risk Score}=\sum \text{Assigned Risk Points}
\]

This can help prioritize cases:
- Low score → Normal processing
- Medium score → Additional verification
- High score → Underwriter review
Important
 These are example points for a prototype, not real insurance industry underwriting thresholds. Actual medical and financial risk models require approval from qualified underwriting and actuarial teams.

10. Questions your manager may ask
Q1. Why did you select the insurance domain?

Because insurance involves real business processes such as customer onboarding, policy management, underwriting, premium calculation and claims. We can build a project that solves a specific problem within these processes.

Q2. Why do we need AI? Can't Java do everything?

Java can handle structured validation and business rules. AI and OCR can help read unstructured documents, extract information and identify possible discrepancies. Both technologies have different responsibilities.

Q3. What is the most important calculation?

For the underwriting prototype, customer age, BMI, policy coverage and configurable risk scoring are useful examples. Premium calculations can also be included if approved pricing rules are available.

Q4. Will AI approve or reject the insurance application?

No. AI will assist with document analysis and risk identification. The authorized underwriter will make the final decision.

Q5. Is this a real production system?

No. This is a proposed project idea. We would first develop a prototype using synthetic data and approved sample business rules.
